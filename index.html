01 / Understand the idea

What does 2FA actually mean?

Two-factor authentication checks two different kinds of proof that it is you. A common example is your password plus a code from an authenticator app on your phone.

Something you know

Your password

A secret you remember or keep in a password manager.

+
Something you have

Your authenticator

An app on your device that creates a sign-in code.

Two passwords do not count as two factors: both are something you know. Another factor type is something you are, such as a fingerprint.

Concepts: NIST's multi-factor authentication guidance.

02 / Know why it matters

A stolen password should not be the whole story.

With a second factor, someone who steals your password still has another barrier to get past. It reduces risk, but does not stop every scam. Scammers can still try to trick you into sharing a code or approving a request.

You may also see the names 2-Step Verification or multi-factor authentication (MFA). MFA means two or more factor types; 2FA uses two.
