The three main research sources

  1. NIST: Multi-Factor Authentication

    The National Institute of Standards and Technology explains factor types, why another factor adds a barrier, and why phishing-resistant methods matter.

    Used for: the meaning of 2FA and the method comparison.

  2. FTC: Use Two-Factor Authentication To Protect Your Accounts

    The Federal Trade Commission gives practical consumer advice about stolen passwords, verification methods, and code-sharing scams.

    Used for: everyday safety habits and the limits of text-message codes.

  3. Google: Turn on 2-Step Verification

    The service provider documents its own setup process and available sign-in options. This is the reference to revisit if Google's menus change.

    Used for: the Google setup path, prompts, and passkey note.

Extra help with recovery

Google: Sign in with backup codes explains how to create, store, and use backup codes.

About the images and code
